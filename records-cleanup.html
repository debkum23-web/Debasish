Records Cleanup Automation

Product Owner perspective on eliminating stale inventory noise

Product Ownership Process Automation Data Quality Stakeholder Management Power Automate

Context

Inventory reports were cluttered with records that were inactive or untouched for over a year. Stakeholders struggled to analyze data or make decisions because obsolete items distorted metrics and priorities.

Problem

Stale records remained visible to business and compliance teams, creating noise, inflating counts, and slowing decisions. Manual cleanup was ad hoc, inconsistent, and rarely prioritized.

Goal

Find the stale records automatically, ask their owners to confirm before anything is removed, and retire what's confirmed obsolete — so the inventory only shows records people can actually act on.

Stakeholders

  • Record owners
  • Business unit leads
  • Compliance / governance partners
  • Data & reporting teams
  • IT / platform admins

Decisions That Shaped It

Explicit approval, not auto-delete

Owners confirm before anything is removed — two reminders, then a timeout. It costs a 30-day delay versus auto-deleting, but nobody gets surprised by an automated process silently deciding what's obsolete.

12-month threshold, not 6

Slower cleanup, but fewer false positives — a shorter window would have flagged records that were just quiet, not actually stale.

Detection first, automation later

Phase 1 only finds and flags stale records. Auto-decommissioning after approval is Phase 3 — proving the detection logic was right mattered more than moving fast on deletion.

Audit trail on every action

Storage overhead, but it's what let compliance sign off on an automated cleanup process instead of treating it as a black box.

What I'd Point To as Product Work

  • Quantifying the noise before proposing automation — mapped how much stale data was distorting reporting and decision latency, so the case for building this wasn't a hunch.
  • Choosing accountability over speed. An approval loop with owner confirmation is slower than auto-delete, but it's the difference between a tool people trust and one they route around.
  • Sequencing detection before deletion. Phase 1 proves what's actually stale before Phase 3 gets the power to remove anything automatically — a mistake in detection logic is cheap to fix before that point, expensive after.
  • Treating the audit trail as a design requirement, not an afterthought, because compliance sign-off decides whether an automated cleanup process gets to run at all.

Success Criteria Defined Upfront

  • 85%+ of flagged stale records resolved
  • 70%+ owner approval rate on confirmation requests
  • Full audit log on every detect / notify / decommission action

Rollout

P1: Detection · P2: MVP + owner notifications · P3: Auto-decommission on approval · P4: Reporting feed
