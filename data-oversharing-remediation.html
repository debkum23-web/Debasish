01

Problem

A high-severity data incident surfaced: content on our SharePoint platform was exposed to far more people than it should have been. I led the same-day containment, coordinating Legal, HR, Audit, and Engineering, none of whom reported to me, and ran the root-cause analysis.

Containing the incident was the easy part to explain. The harder question came right after it: if this happened once, where else was it happening? On a platform of 30–40K sites and roughly 10TB of content, nobody could answer that, and the only way we knew how to find exposure was to wait for an incident and handle it case by case.

02

Product Decision

A one-off incident triggered one question: what if this happens again, and how can it be detected and resolved as part of a framework rather than by teams handling it case by case? I decided to build the framework, not just close the incident.

01

A repeatable framework, not another incident response

Find exposure proactively across every site, instead of waiting for the next one to be reported.

02

Built in-house

We designed our own solution that scans all sites and identifies overexposure, so detection fit how our platform is actually set up.

03

Site owners confirm before anything is removed

The owner is the person who knows whether an exposed item holds sensitive content. So the process asks them, and if it does, they either remove the over-exposure or take the confidential item down.

04

Design who does what, not just what gets scanned

Detection is only useful if someone acts on it. I designed the process end to end: who identifies, who reaches out, who confirms, and who validates.

03

Stakeholder Alignment

Nobody in this process reported to me. It worked because each team had a clearly defined part in it, designed with them in partnership.

01

Legal, HR, Audit, and Engineering — the incident

Same-day containment meant getting four functions moving together on a problem none of them owned alone, while I ran the root-cause analysis.

02

Leadership — the program

I turned the incident into a case for a company-wide detection program, and leadership backed it.

03

M365 admins — identification

They own running the identification and producing the report.

04

Comms team — outreach and validation

They take the report, reach out to the site owners, and validate that the next steps were taken. I designed the hand-off between the M365 admins and this team.

05

Site owners — the sensitivity call

They confirm whether exposed items hold sensitive content, and act if they do.

04

Technical Approach

An in-house solution scans all sites and identifies overexposed items. The M365 admins run the identification and share the report with the comms team, who take it from there.

From scan to a confirmed, validated fix

The boundary I designed in

Detection is automated and centralized. Judging whether content is actually sensitive is not. That call sits with the person who owns the site, and the comms team checks that what was agreed actually happened.

05

Outcome

1,000+

Previously undetected exposure risks remediated across the platform.

30–40K sites

Scanned, about 10TB of content, instead of waiting for the next incident to reveal a gap.

One incident

Became a repeatable framework: exposure is found, confirmed by the owner, fixed, and validated, no longer handled team by team.

06

Ongoing Ownership

This is a running program, not a closed project. The report still goes to the comms team, and they continue to validate that owners took the agreed action.

  • The process is mine. I designed who does what and how the teams hand off to each other, and I keep it working.
  • We've since improved how scanning and reporting work so that they are closer to real time, which shortens the gap between exposure and discovery.
