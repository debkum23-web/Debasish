LLM-Powered SharePoint Audit Analyzer

Product Owner / AI Solutions Architect

AI/LLM RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) Vector Embeddings SharePoint Compliance Streamlit Product Strategy

The Problem

An employee accidentally accessed a confidential SharePoint site and modified a file. HR and leadership wanted answers fast: who touched it, what changed, when, and how bad was it. In practice that meant handing it to whoever on the technical team could read SharePoint audit logs — raw JSON, cryptic event codes — and even a simple incident like this one took 4-6 hours of manual log review to answer.

The real problem was the dependency, not any one investigation. HR and leadership couldn't read the logs themselves, so every incident meant pulling someone off their actual work to go dig through JSON. What should have taken minutes stretched into hours, and the people who needed the answer fastest were the ones furthest from being able to get it on their own.

The Goal

Let HR and leadership investigate incidents themselves — no JSON, no waiting on the technical team. Just plain questions: "Who accessed this file on Friday?" "What changed?" "Which accounts touched this site?" — answered in minutes instead of hours, with non-technical leaders as the first line of response instead of the last.

How I Approached This as a Product Owner

Start with Real Users

I shadowed security analysts doing their actual work. Watched them hunt through logs, copy JSON snippets, switch between tools. Saw where they got stuck. Learned that they weren't struggling with anything exotic—just the same basic questions repeatedly: Who accessed this? What happened when? I turned those observations into concrete requirements.

Prove It Works First

Before building anything, I needed to know: can AI actually understand audit logs well enough to give good answers? I prototyped the architecture—vector embeddings, semantic search, LLM generation—on test scenarios. Found it was 95% accurate. That confidence meant I could commit to the vision without overselling.

Ship What Matters First

I prioritized ruthlessly. MVP 1 was just the natural-language chat interface, because that alone solves the core pain — hours to minutes. Trend analysis, anomaly detection, incident correlation, all of it waits until I see how teams actually use the first thing.

Measure Everything

I defined success metrics upfront: How fast are investigations now? How accurate are the answers? Are people actually using it? These weren't vanity metrics—they directly told me if the product was solving the problem. When metrics went up, I knew I was on the right track.

Build Guardrails for AI

AI is powerful but imperfect. I built in confidence scoring, verification workflows for sensitive findings, and audit trails. I made sure human judgment stays in the loop for high-stakes decisions. Security teams need to trust the tool, and that means being honest about what it can and can't do.

Be Honest About Limitations

Vector search captures meaning well but isn't perfect — it's semantic, not syntactic, and it can miss edge cases. I told the security team that upfront, no overselling. When something later needed fixing, they came to me with the feedback instead of quietly losing trust in the tool.

How It Works

Three pieces, each doing one job:

Understand the Data

It loads SharePoint audit logs from CSV and parses the nested JSON inside — which gets messy fast, all technical codes and buried fields — and pulls out what actually matters: who did what, when, and where.

Convert to Semantic Understanding

Each log entry gets converted into a semantic vector — a mathematical representation of what it means — and stored in ChromaDB, a database built for fast similarity search. When you ask a question, the system finds the log entries closest in meaning to what you asked, even if you didn't use the exact words in the log.

AI-Powered Answers

When you ask a question in the chat, the system finds the most relevant logs, hands them to an LLM (GPT-4), and asks it to answer your question based on that context. This is "RAG"—Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Your answer is grounded in real data, not hallucinated.

Instant Insights

Beyond answering questions, the system automatically generates statistics: most active users, common operations, permission changes, suspicious activities. Everything you'd normally manually compile—but instant and consistent.

What This Means for Your Organization

Speed: From Hours to Minutes

A breach alert comes in on Friday afternoon. Instead of a 4-6 hour investigation eating someone's evening, there are answers in about 5 minutes. The window where you don't know what happened — which is the risky part — shrinks to almost nothing.

No Expertise Required

A junior analyst can answer the same questions as your SharePoint expert. Nobody needs to parse JSON or know what event code 4662 means — they ask in plain English and get an answer.

Trust the Results

Everyone using the same tool means everyone gets the same answer — no more one team catching something another missed. And because every answer is grounded in the actual audit data instead of guesswork, the results hold up to scrutiny.

Never Outgrow It

Whether you have 10,000 logs or 10 million, the system handles it instantly. No waiting for data collection. No timeout errors. Your compliance team can investigate any incident, any time period, across your entire organization—right now.

What I Actually Did

  • Watched People Work: Sat with security analysts while they investigated real incidents. Watched them get stuck. Watched them find workarounds. Learned more in those few hours than any document could teach me.
  • Picked Battles Wisely: Not every feature idea matters. I mapped out what would move the needle (MVP: ask questions in English) vs. nice-to-haves (advanced analytics). Got 80% of the value with 20% of the work.
  • Built in Safety: AI can be amazing, but it can also be confidently wrong. I designed guardrails: confidence scores on answers, human review for sensitive findings, detailed audit trails for compliance. Security teams needed to trust this, and that requires rigor.
  • Defined Success Upfront: How do you know if this product works? I said: investigations drop from 4-6 hours to under 10 minutes. Accuracy is above 90%. Teams are using it daily. Then I built metrics to track exactly that.
  • Talked Straight About Limits: Told security teams exactly what this system could and couldn't do. Vector search catches semantic meaning beautifully but isn't perfect. AI reasoning is generally solid but can miss edge cases. Honesty builds trust.
  • Made It Feel Natural: The interface isn't some clunky tool. It's a chat. You ask questions like you'd ask a person. The system even explains why it pulled certain logs. Details like this matter—they make people actually use the thing.
  • Stayed Flexible: We shipped the MVP—basic natural language queries. Then watched how teams actually used it. Added filtering when they asked for it. Added trend analysis because they needed it. The roadmap came from reality, not guesswork.

How We Got Here

The Spark: Watched a security team spend 6 hours on a simple compliance check. Realized this was a solvable problem if we applied AI the right way.

MVP 1 (Built): A conversational interface where you ask questions in English. "Who accessed this file?" "What happened last week?" Simple, powerful, immediately useful.

MVP 2 (Built): Added dashboard summaries, trend analysis, and permission change tracking. The system now proactively surfaces patterns, not just answers questions.

Next Chapter: Incident correlation—connect events across multiple logs. Automation—trigger workflows when suspicious patterns emerge. The vision keeps growing based on what teams actually need.

What I Learned

The AI part of this — RAG, embeddings, an LLM answering from real data instead of guessing — turned out to be the easy part. It's well-trodden ground by now. The harder part was the same product work it always is: sitting with security analysts long enough to see what they actually got stuck on, saying no to features that would've looked impressive on a slide but didn't move the 4-6-hour number, and being straight with people about where the AI falls short instead of letting them find out the hard way.

Being upfront about limitations paid off more than any single feature did. I told the security team exactly where vector search falls short and built verification into the high-stakes paths. Because of that, when something did need fixing later, they brought it to me instead of quietly deciding not to trust the tool. That's the difference between people using something because they were told to and using it because it actually works for them.
