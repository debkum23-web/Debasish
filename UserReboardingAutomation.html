Shipping the highest-pain piece first, and holding off on the rest

User Re-boarding Access Automation

Power Automate SharePoint Online Microsoft Graph API Process Automation User Experience

Context

When employees re-joined the company, they encountered access issues with SharePoint sites and files that had been previously shared with them during their earlier tenure. Their old account traces remained in the system, creating permission conflicts.

Problem

Re-joining employees had to raise helpdesk tickets and wait days for resolution. The manual two-step process required: (1) IT support to manually identify and remove old account traces from sites, and (2) contacting site owners to re-share access. This time-consuming, multi-handoff workflow delayed productivity and created frustration for re-joiners, helpdesk teams, and site owners alike.

Goal

Get the days-long manual process down to minutes. Take the helpdesk out of step 1 entirely (the cleanup), make step 2 (re-sharing requests) less painful, and make sure people rejoining the company can actually work on day one.

Users & Stakeholders

  • Re-joining employees
  • IT support team
  • HR operations
  • Site owners & administrators
  • Business unit managers

What I'd Point To as Product Work

Measured the pain before proposing a fix

Shadowed the helpdesk doing the manual cleanup and pulled ticket data to put a real number on it: 3-5 days average wait. That number, not an opinion, is what got IT, HR, and site owners aligned fast.

Shipped the highest-pain piece first, deliberately

MVP 1 automated the cleanup step only — the highest-pain, lowest-effort piece. I held off automating the re-sharing step until the numbers justified it, instead of pitching the full vision up front.

Let a small win earn the bigger ask

Shipping something small that obviously worked bought the trust to propose the larger HR-integration phase later. Pitching the whole roadmap on day one would have been a harder sell.

Solution

Designed and delivered a Power Automate workflow that:

Identifies Old Account Traces

Scans every SharePoint site and library for permissions still tied to the employee's old account — the traces nobody remembers to clean up by hand.

Automates Permission Cleanup (Step 1)

Removes those old traces automatically across sites, libraries, and files. The helpdesk step that used to take days now takes minutes.

Notifies Stakeholders

Confirms back to the employee and IT support once cleanup is done, so nobody's left wondering whether it actually ran.

Compliance Logging

Logs every action the automation takes, so governance can trace exactly what changed and when — not just take our word for it.

Outcomes

Days to Minutes

What used to be 3-5 days of waiting on the helpdesk now runs as a few minutes of automated processing.

85% Ticket Reduction

Most re-boarding access tickets simply stopped arriving, which gave IT support their time back.

Same-Day Access

People rejoining the company get their access on day one instead of spending their first week chasing it.

Zero Manual Errors

The cleanup runs the same way every time. The permission mistakes that came with doing it by hand are gone.

Details Worth Knowing

  • Edge cases came from workshops with IT and site owners, not assumptions — partial access, guest users, and external sharing all needed their own handling.
  • Acceptance criteria were defined jointly with IT and security, so "done" wasn't a subjective call on either side.
  • Peak re-boarding volume tracks re-hiring seasons, which shaped when the automation needed to hold up under load.

Evolution Journey

Before: Manual two-step process taking 3-5 days with multiple helpdesk handoffs

Phase 1: Process observation and data-driven problem quantification

Phase 2: MVP 1 - Automated Step 1 (remove old traces of user account) reducing wait time to minutes

Phase 3: MVP 2 - Planned - Enhanced Step 2 with intelligent re-sharing and site owner notifications

Phase 4: Full integration with HR onboarding for proactive access provisioning

Key Takeaways

The thing that made this project work was sitting with the helpdesk team and watching them do the cleanup by hand. Once I could say "this takes 3-5 days and here are the ticket numbers to prove it," getting IT, site owners, and HR aligned was straightforward — nobody defends a process that bad once it's measured. We shipped the automated cleanup first because it was the highest-pain, lowest-effort piece, and held off on automating the re-sharing step until we could see whether the numbers justified it. That restraint turned out to matter: shipping something small that obviously worked bought us the trust to propose the bigger HR integration later. If I had pitched the whole vision up front, I don't think it would have been approved.
