GenAI-Assisted Ticket Analysis & Feature Discovery

Using AI to transform raw support data into structured product insights—reducing manual analysis by 80% and enabling faster, data-driven feature prioritization.

GenAI / Excel Copilot Product Discovery Data-Driven Decisions Feature Prioritization Incident Reduction

Executive Summary

We were sitting on 18 months of support tickets — more than 1,200 — and our roadmap was still being driven by whoever complained loudest. I used Excel Copilot to classify and theme the whole backlog. What would have taken weeks of manual reading took a few hours, and it surfaced two feature gaps big enough to build.

Both features shipped. More importantly, the roadmap conversation changed: instead of debating anecdotes, we were looking at ticket counts.

At a Glance

  • 1,200+ tickets, 18 months of backlog
  • 12 theme clusters identified
  • 2 features validated by ticket volume, not opinion

From raw tickets to shipped features

How I Ran the Analysis

I used Excel Copilot to classify and theme the raw ticket descriptions — 65% feature requests, 25% defects, 10% enhancements — and cluster them into 12 product areas. Two of those clusters were big enough to justify building: Copy Site Pages (310 tickets) and Re-boarding Automation (185 tickets). Every AI-generated category got a manual spot-check before I trusted it; the AI found the pattern, I made the call on what to build.

Two of the actual prompts I used

Analyze the Description column in the Support_Tickets table and create a new Issue Category column grouping similar user problems.
Using the Issue Category column, identify trends and highlight issues that show sustained growth over time.

Time Investment

Manual read-through of 18 months of tickets would have taken 4-6 weeks. The Copilot-assisted pass took 6-8 hours plus validation time.

What I'd Point To as Product Work

  • Choosing volume over opinion. Instead of debating who complained loudest, I let ticket counts settle which two features actually deserved a build slot.
  • Keeping the judgment call mine. Copilot did the pattern-finding across 1,200+ tickets; every AI-generated category still got a manual spot-check before I trusted it or put it in front of stakeholders.
  • Saying no to ten ideas to say yes to two. The analysis surfaced 12 distinct theme clusters — only two had the volume and clear enough scope to justify building.
  • Turning an unstructured backlog into a number a skeptic can't easily argue with. "310 tickets are asking for this" ended prioritization debates that "I think users want this" never could.

Key Takeaways

Honestly, when I started this I expected to spend weeks reading tickets and still miss things. A few hours with Copilot gave me structured themes, sentiment, and a clean split between feature requests and defects. The part that surprised me was how it changed the prioritization conversations. When I proposed Copy Site Pages, I wasn't arguing a hunch — I could point to 310 tickets. Re-boarding Automation had 185 behind it. It's hard to argue with that, and nobody really tried. The AI didn't decide anything; it just got me to the evidence fast enough for the evidence to matter.
