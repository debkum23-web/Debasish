AI Product Owner Learning & Mock Work

This is where I work through AI product ideas I haven't shipped — mock projects, thought through the way I'd actually approach them if they were real:

🤖 AI-Powered SharePoint Content Assistant

Concept: A RAG assistant that finds and summarizes the right document in a SharePoint knowledge base most people can't search their way through.

PO Focus: First proving the real problem is information overload, not bad search UX. Then narrowing the MVP to semantic search plus a one-line summary, and holding it to one metric: how much faster someone gets to an answer.

RAG Architecture LLM Integration User Research Product Strategy

📊 Intelligent Data Governance Agent

Concept: An agent that watches SharePoint governance signals in real time and flags what a human would otherwise catch too late — capacity, anomalies, compliance gaps.

PO Focus: Phasing the autonomy deliberately: it only flags anomalies at first, then it can recommend a fix, and only once both are trusted does it get to act on its own. Trust gets earned in that order, not assumed.

AI Agents Governance Product Roadmapping ROI Metrics

🏃 Automated Process Discovery Tool (Mock)

Concept: A tool that reads process logs and points at where automation would actually save time, instead of an ops team guessing.

PO Focus: The interesting question isn't the tech — UiPath and Automation Anywhere already do process mining. It's whether Power Platform's install base is a real wedge or just a nice-to-have, and I'd want that answered before writing a line of code.

Process Mining AI-Driven Insights GTM Strategy Competitive Analysis

💬 AI-Powered Stakeholder Alignment Assistant

Concept: A tool that reads scattered stakeholder feedback and surfaces where people actually agree — and where they're quietly not.

PO Focus: This one's personal — I've sat in enough meetings that were really just three people who hadn't realized yet that they disagreed. I'd validate it by whether decisions actually get made faster, not whether the meeting just felt more organized.

📖 AI Product Strategy Study

Focus: Working through how ChatGPT, Copilot, and GitHub Copilot actually got built and shipped — not the marketing version, the product decisions underneath.

Learning Outcomes: What's different about discovery when a product's output is probabilistic, how to set metrics for something without one right answer, and where the responsible-AI conversation stops being theoretical and starts being a shipping decision.

🔄 Prompt Engineering & Experimentation

Focus: Running the experiments myself — prompt tuning, RAG vs. fine-tuning, where cost and latency start fighting quality — instead of taking an engineer's word for what's feasible.

PO Relevance: I want to be the PO who can tell the difference between a feature request that's a prompt-tuning afternoon and one that's a genuinely hard model problem. That's what separates a realistic roadmap from a hopeful one.
